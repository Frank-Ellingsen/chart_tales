There are numbers that are dead to us.

He did not see them, of course.

He saw columns.

He saw percentages.

He saw Actual, Budget and Forecast.

What he did not see was the story.

That would require three spirits.

One from **Data Past**.

One from **Data Present**.

And one from **Data Yet to Come**.

And all three had something to show him.

# The 24-Day LinkedIn Series

## ACT I — Opening the Windows

### Day 1 — The Bar Chart
**Question:** Which is biggest?

🎄 **DAY 1 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

The first window opens.

Behind it stands perhaps the most familiar figure in data visualisation: the **bar chart**.

Simple? Yes.

Boring? Only when the question is.

A bar chart is excellent when we want to compare categories and see, almost instantly, which is largest, smallest or unexpectedly different.

Today's Christmas question: **Which Christmas tradition is the most popular?**

The chart does not need decoration, shadows or clever effects. Its strength lies in clarity.

A good data story does not always begin with complexity. Sometimes it begins with one honest comparison.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 2 — The Column Chart
**Question:** How much?

🎄 **DAY 2 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

Another window opens. Today, the categories stand upright.

The **column chart** is useful when we want to compare values across a limited number of categories or discrete periods.

Christmas question: **How is seasonal spending distributed across gifts, food, travel and decorations?**

The tallest column naturally attracts attention. That makes sorting, scale and colour important. Highlight everything, and nothing is highlighted.

**Use colour to direct the eye, not merely to decorate the chart.**

The chart shows the amounts. The title should tell us why those amounts matter.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 3 — The Dot Plot
**Theme:** Less ink. More information.

🎄 **DAY 3 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

What happens when we remove the bars and leave only their endpoints?

We get a **dot plot**.

Christmas question: **How does seasonal spending per household compare across countries?**

The position of each dot carries the comparison. With less visual weight than a conventional bar chart, differences can become easier to see.

**Every element should earn its place.**

Gridlines should help us read. Labels should provide context. Colour should guide attention.

Everything else may simply be taking up room at the Christmas table.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 4 — The Lollipop Chart
**Theme:** Decorating the data

🎄 **DAY 4 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

Today's chart looks as though it has dressed for Christmas.

The **lollipop chart** combines a line and a dot to create a visually lighter alternative to a bar chart.

Question: **Which Christmas products sold the most?**

A warning from the spirit of good design: a lollipop chart is not automatically better because it looks different.

Choose it when its lighter appearance supports the story, not simply because it is fashionable.

A new chart should improve understanding. Otherwise, it is only decoration.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 5 — The Line Chart
**Theme:** Following Christmas through time

🎄 **DAY 5 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

With today's window, time enters our story.

The **line chart** connects observations in sequence and allows us to follow movement, direction and rhythm.

Question: **How does seasonal activity develop as Christmas approaches?**

A line chart helps us see more than isolated values. It reveals the journey between them.

But resist adding too many lines. Highlight the line that matters. Let the others provide context.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 6 — The Area Chart
**Theme:** When the story grows

🎄 **DAY 6 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

A line shows change. An **area chart** gives that change visual weight.

Question: **How does the volume of seasonal demand build toward Christmas?**

By filling the space beneath the line, the chart draws attention to magnitude as well as movement.

**Visual impact should strengthen the truth, not stretch it.**

The first six windows are now open.

Tomorrow, the room grows colder.

The first spirit is approaching.

*But numbers have long memories.*

# ACT II — The Ghost of Data Past

### Day 7 — The Slope Chart

🎄 **DAY 7 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

## The Ghost of Data Past

The first spirit takes us backward.

A **slope chart** compares two moments and makes the direction of change immediately visible.

Question: **How has Christmas spending changed since last year?**

Each line connects the past with the present. Some categories rise. Some fall. Some remain almost untouched by time.

Two moments can tell a powerful story, if they are the right two moments.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 8 — The Dumbbell Chart

🎄 **DAY 8 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

Budget and actual have met beneath the mistletoe. They do not always agree.

The **dumbbell chart** compares two values and draws attention to the distance between them.

Question: **Where are the largest gaps between planned and actual Christmas spending?**

One dot represents budget. The other actual. The connecting line shows variance.

For controllers, the important question is not merely **What is the variance?** It is **Why did it occur, and what should we do about it?**

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 9 — The Indexed Line Chart

🎄 **DAY 9 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

Some indicators use euros. Others use units, visitors, orders or hours.

How can we compare their development?

Set each series to the same starting point and follow relative change.

Question: **Which seasonal indicator has grown fastest?**

Indexing changes the question from **Who is biggest?** to **Who has changed most?**

Different question. Different chart. Different story.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 10 — Small Multiples

🎄 **DAY 10 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

Sometimes one chart contains too many competing stories.

**Small multiples** repeat the same chart structure across categories, countries, stores or products.

Question: **How does the Christmas pattern differ from one store to another?**

Same scale. Same period. Same treatment. Different stories.

A chorus of charts can be easier to understand than a single chart where every line is shouting.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 11 — Calendar Heatmap

🎄 **DAY 11 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

When does Christmas really begin?

A **calendar heatmap** places daily values into a familiar calendar structure and uses colour intensity to reveal patterns.

Question: **On which days does seasonal activity accelerate?**

Colour is not the story.

It is the lantern that helps us find it.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 12 — The Bump Chart

🎄 **DAY 12 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

History is not only about quantities. It is also about position.

A **bump chart** follows changes in rank through time.

Question: **Which Christmas traditions, products or films have risen and fallen in popularity?**

Who climbed? Who fell? Who remained firmly at the top?

And so the first spirit departs.

The past has spoken. But our traveller has not yet learned his lesson.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

# ACT III — The Ghost of Data Present

### Day 13 — The Histogram

🎄 **DAY 13 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

The second spirit asks us to look around.

A **histogram** groups numerical observations into intervals and reveals the shape of a distribution.

Question: **What is a typical level of Christmas gift spending?**

The average may provide one answer. The distribution may provide several.

A single number can be convenient. A distribution is often more honest.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 14 — The Box Plot

🎄 **DAY 14 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

What is normal? What is unusual? How much variation hides behind the average?

The **box plot** summarises a distribution through median, quartiles, spread and potential outliers.

Question: **How consistent are seasonal sales across stores?**

An outlier is not automatically an error. It may be the beginning of the most interesting part of the story.

Investigate it.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 15 — The Scatter Plot

🎄 **DAY 15 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

Do Christmas advertising and seasonal sales move together?

A **scatter plot** helps explore the relationship between two numerical variables.

The spirit offers a warning:

**Correlation is not proof of causation.**

The scatter plot does not always give us the answer. Sometimes its greatest value is showing us which question to ask next.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 16 — The Bubble Chart

🎄 **DAY 16 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

Two dimensions have become three.

Sales. Margin. Volume.

A **bubble chart** extends a scatter plot by using marker size for an additional variable.

More dimensions do not automatically create more insight.

Use the third variable when it changes the interpretation. Otherwise, let the scatter plot breathe.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 17 — The Treemap

🎄 **DAY 17 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

What makes up the Christmas shopping basket?

A **treemap** uses nested rectangles to show categories, subcategories and their share of a whole.

Question: **How is Christmas expenditure divided between gifts, food, travel, decorations and entertainment?**

Use it when overall composition and hierarchy matter more than precise ranking.

The Christmas tree may have many branches. The reader should still be able to see its shape.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 18 — The Waterfall Chart

🎄 **DAY 18 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

We began with a budget. We ended with an actual result.

What happened between them?

The **waterfall chart** explains how positive and negative contributions move a value from start to finish.

Budget → Increased sales → Higher transport costs → Seasonal staffing → Improved product mix → Actual.

For finance and controlling, this transforms a variance from a static number into a sequence of drivers.

That is the difference between reporting and explanation.

The second spirit fades.

**What happens next?**

# ACT IV — The Ghost of Data Yet to Come

### Day 19 — The Forecast Chart

🎄 **DAY 19 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

The final spirit does not speak.

It points toward a line continuing beyond the latest observation.

Question: **Where is Christmas demand heading?**

A forecast should distinguish **what happened** from **what we expect may happen**.

The future should be visible, but never disguised as fact.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 20 — The Fan Chart

🎄 **DAY 20 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

A forecast is rarely one inevitable number.

A **fan chart** places uncertainty around a central forecast.

Question: **What range of Christmas demand should we prepare for?**

Instead of asking whether one forecast is exactly correct, ask whether we are prepared for the range of plausible outcomes.

Uncertainty is not a weakness in the analysis.

Hiding it is.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 21 — The Scenario Chart

🎄 **DAY 21 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

The future branches.

Optimistic. Expected. Pessimistic.

A **scenario chart** compares plausible futures without pretending any one is guaranteed.

The question changes from **What do we think will happen?** to **What will we do if something else happens?**

A useful forecast informs expectation.

A useful scenario prepares action.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 22 — The Sankey Diagram

🎄 **DAY 22 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

Some stories concern size. Others concern movement.

A **Sankey diagram** follows flows between stages or categories.

Question: **Where does the Christmas budget go?**

Use it when movement itself is the story.

Too many paths, however, can turn the result into a tangle of Christmas lights.

*But numbers have long memories.*

---

### Day 23 — The Connected Scatterplot

🎄 **DAY 23 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

Today, two measures travel through time together.

A **connected scatterplot** combines relationship, position and sequence.

Question: **How have seasonal sales and profitability moved together?**

Used carefully, it can create something unusual:

A plot with movement.

A relationship with memory.

A chart with a beginning, a middle and an end.

Tomorrow, the storyteller becomes the story.

*But numbers have long memories.*

# DAY 24 — CHRISTMAS EVE
# The Storyteller Becomes the Story

🎄 **DAY 24 | A Christmas Chart Carol**

For twenty-three days, we have asked charts to tell us stories.

Today, we turn the chart upon the storyteller himself.

## Charles Dickens: A Life in Data

**1812–1870**

A life and literary career presented as a cinematic visual timeline.

Major works can appear along the chronology, including:

- *The Pickwick Papers*
- *Oliver Twist*
- *David Copperfield*
- *Bleak House*
- *A Tale of Two Cities*
- *Great Expectations*

And glowing at the heart of the visual:

## 🎄 1843 — *A Christmas Carol*

The timeline continues toward 1870, but does not finish neatly.

It fades into Victorian mist beside Dickens's final unfinished work:

### *The Mystery of Edwin Drood*
**Unfinished.**

The data ends.

The stories do not.

For twenty-four days, we have explored bars, lines, dots, distributions, relationships, flows, forecasts and uncertainty.

But none of them was really the subject.

The subject was the **story**.

A chart can show us what happened.

A good chart can help us understand what is happening.

And sometimes, a chart allows us to glimpse what may happen next.

**Past.**

**Present.**

**Yet to Come.**

Perhaps Dickens would have understood data visualisation rather well.

After all...
